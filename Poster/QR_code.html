01 / The idea

Confidence needs
something behind it.

An agent can recommend a product convincingly. RAMIFY asks the next question: what evidence supports that recommendation, and is this agent actually allowed to act?

Understand the request

An optional local language model helps interpret free-form requests and explain completed results. Product trust is determined by explicit rules and evidence.

Check before acting

Identity, batch standing, seller authority, evidence and claims are assessed consistently. Buyer policy can add restrictions, while hard stops remain in force.

Keep the reasoning

A sealed receipt connects the product, checks, policy, reasons and permitted actions. Later review and simulated transactions remain linked to their supporting records.

The central distinction

“What does the evidence say about this product?” and “What may this buyer do?” are recorded separately. The same product facts can support different buyer actions.

03 / Inside RATIFY

Seven questions
behind the outcome.

RATIFY evaluates the evidence against the packaged demonstration policy. Each check records its finding and reason; a primary reason never erases the other findings.

01

Identity resolution

Does the identifier resolve to exactly one known subject? An unresolved identity is incomplete, so the system cannot treat it as a clean assessment.

02

Recall & advisory standing

Check the applicable product or batch status. An active recall blocks action; an active advisory routes the case to review.

03

Seller authority

Is the seller authorised for the category? Revoked authority is a hard stop. Unverified authority is recorded as a finding without inventing an allegation.

04

Mandatory evidence

Are the evidence types required by this category present? Missing documents remain missing information; absence cannot count as a pass.

05

Evidence validity & integrity

Check dates and record status, hash the actual artefact bytes, verify issuer signatures and check the signed binding between evidence and the assessed subject.

06

Claims & category requirements

Check required claims, their status and the issuer’s authority to make them. Claims must be backed by the relevant evidence; rejected or revoked claims can force a stop.

07

Conflicting information

Compare material claims from different issuers. A conflict is retained for review. The demo’s EPA/DHA comparison uses a 5 mg tolerance per component; other ingredient formats use normalised comparison.

Integrity: have the bytes changed?

A matching hash and valid issuer signature support the authenticity of the recorded artefact. Signed metadata binds it to the relevant subject, scope and claims.

Validity: is this evidence usable?

An intact document may still be expired, withdrawn, not yet in effect or irrelevant to this batch. Cryptographic integrity alone does not make it valid evidence.

Source: policy_pack_demo_v1.json, ratify/checks.py and ratify/evidence_binding.py. These are simplified demonstration rules.

Identity & context

Which decision is this?

receipt_id names the record; subject_ref identifies the product. assessment_context distinguishes a decision from a purchase assessment, so an old browsing result cannot silently become checkout authority.

1

Canonicalise the payload

Sort JSON keys; use compact UTF-8 encoding. Exclude only payload_hash and signature. Floating-point values are rejected; money uses integer cents.

2

Hash the canonical bytes

Compute SHA-256 and store sha256: followed by the 64-character hexadecimal digest.

3

Sign the digest

The backend signs the raw 32-byte digest with Ed25519 and stores the signature in Base64. Verification needs trusted public-key material.

Source: receipt/builder.py, crypto/canonical.py and crypto/sign.py.

01 · Receipt integrity

The backend recomputes the hash, verifies the signature, resolves issuer references and checks scope. A matching hash alone is insufficient: someone could edit content and recompute an unsigned hash.

02 · Current purchase authority

The prototype adds a one-hour receipt window. A historically intact record may be too old to authorise a new purchase. Integrity and freshness answer different questions.

03 · Action & transaction checks

The Action Gate checks the requested action against sealed permissions. Checkout also binds the product, actor, quantity and total, and rejects reused or superseded authority.

07 / Human accountability

Review adds a record.
It preserves the past.

A human decision has its own place in the audit trail. The original machine result remains available, together with the reason for any permitted, one-time authorisation.

01 →

Machine assessment

The system records the objective posture, actor restrictions, evidence findings and a sealed receipt.

02 →

Eligible human review

A person inspects the reasons and may decline, or authorise an eligible case once with an explicit rationale.

03 →

Linked successor

A system-sealed Human Receipt links back through supersedes_receipt. The original product truth is retained.

04 ↗

Verified next step

The authorised action is checked again. A simulated order or requisition links each line to its supporting receipt.

Review is a controlled exception

One-time authorisation is restricted to eligible review cases. Transaction authority is tied to the sealed context and purchase style; procurement review does not create a consumer checkout path.

Some findings cannot be overridden

A recall or other hard stop cannot be approved away. Incomplete evidence cannot receive purchase authority. The system requires a new assessment when the underlying information changes.

Lineage, not replacement

Preserving the machine result and linking a human successor makes it possible to inspect what the system found, what the person decided and which record authorised the action.

10 / The useful questions

A little more clarity.

For visitors who scanned the poster and want to understand what the project actually demonstrates.

Does the AI make the trust decision?

The optional model interprets language and explains sealed results. Deterministic RESOLVE and RATIFY checks, actor policy and the Action Gate determine the product posture and permitted action.

Why are warning and review both orange?

They both need attention, but they grant different capabilities. allow_with_warning can permit an action subject to the actor’s rules. hold and escalate stop automated buying. The text label and machine posture carry the decision; colour alone does not.

Does a valid signature prove the product is safe?

It verifies that the recorded content matches a signature under a trusted key. The evidence still needs valid dates, relevant scope, authorised issuers and appropriate policy checks. The supplied evidence is synthetic and certifies no real product.

Can a receipt be edited?

A file can be changed. Recomputing the hash and checking the signature makes unauthorised content changes detectable. This is tamper evidence, not a promise that storage is impossible to alter or that signing keys cannot be compromised.

Can a human override a recalled product?

No. Hard stops remain blocked. Human review can authorise eligible cases once, with a linked record and rationale. Incomplete evidence does not become purchase authority through approval.
